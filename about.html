Alek Silenko

Owner & Painter, True Painting MN

I'm 19, born and raised in Plymouth, and I've been painting since I was a teenager — on real job sites, learning it properly before I ever charged anyone for it. True Painting MN is me: the person who gives you the estimate, the person on the ladder, and the person you hand the check to when it's done.

Let's get the obvious question out of the way

I'm 19. You probably worked that out from the phone call, and I'd rather put it in writing than have it be a thing you're wondering about while I'm standing in your living room.

Here's what I'd say to that. I've been painting since I was young — not as a summer job, but on real job sites, doing the sanding and the cut-in and the cleanup that nobody photographs. I learned it from people who did it for a living and who had no patience for shortcuts. By the time most people my age were figuring out what they wanted to do, I already knew how to prep a wall properly, and I'd already been told off enough times for not doing it well enough that it stuck.

What you're hiring is the hands that do the work. Not a salesperson who quotes your house and then hands it to whoever's free that week.

Why I started my own thing

Working for other people, the same pattern kept showing up: the prep got squeezed because the schedule was tight, and the person who'd sold the job wasn't the person who had to live with the result. Nobody was being malicious. It's just what happens when there are three layers between the homeowner and the brush.

I wanted to do it the other way. One person, one job at a time, and my own name attached to the outcome. If your walls look great, that's on me. If something's wrong, that's also on me, and there's no one else for me to point at.

How I'd tell you to hire any painter — including me

Ask for the price in writing before anything starts. Don't pay in full up front. Walk the finished job with them in good light before you hand over the balance. Ask them what they'll do about the patching and how many coats you're getting, and see whether the answer is specific or vague.

I do all of those by default, because they're what I'd want if it were my mom's house. But you should ask them of everyone you're considering. A painter who gets defensive about any of it has told you something useful.

What I actually believe about this work

Prep is the job. Rolling paint is the easy part and it's the part everyone sees. Whether your walls still look right in five years is decided entirely by what happened before the finish coat.

Say the real number. One written price, and it doesn't move unless you add work. If I find something behind your couch that neither of us knew about, I stop and talk to you before I do anything about it. Nobody should ever get a bill with a surprise on it.

Say no when the answer is no. I don't do cabinets, exteriors, ceilings, doors, or major drywall. I could take that money and give you a mediocre result. I'd rather tell you who to call.

Your house isn't a job site. You live there. It gets covered before anything opens and it gets cleaned up at the end of every day, not just the last one.

Faith, and why it's on this page

Church is a real part of my life, not a line on a website. I'm mentioning it for one reason: it's where the way I run this comes from. Do what you said you'd do. Charge what you said you'd charge. Don't take advantage of someone who can't tell whether you cut a corner.

That's it — you'll never hear about it from me on the job, and it makes no difference to how I treat you or what you pay. The one practical effect is that I don't work Sundays. I'd rather tell you that up front than be vague and then cancel on you.

Where I'm starting from — honestly

True Painting MN is new, and I'm not going to dress that up.

I don't have a hundred Google reviews yet, because I haven't done a hundred jobs under my own name yet. I'm not going to buy fake ones or borrow someone else's. What I can offer you instead is this: you'll get more care and attention than you'd get from a company with a full schedule, because right now every single job I do is the one that decides whether this business works. That's not a sales line, it's just the situation.

On insurance: I'm in the process of getting general liability coverage in place, and until it's done I'm not going to claim it. If that's a requirement for your job — and for some homeowners, and most HOAs, it is — ask me directly and I'll tell you exactly where things stand that day.

If being someone's early customer isn't for you, I completely understand. If it is, I'll make it worth your while.

What I'd like from you

If I do good work: tell someone, and leave me a review. For a business like mine that's not a nice-to-have, it's the whole thing.

If I get something wrong: tell me before you tell the internet. I'll fix it.
