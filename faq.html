The basics

Cost, timing and what to expect

How much does it cost to paint a room in the Twin Cities?

For a standard bedroom with 8-foot ceilings, walls only, most homeowners in the west metro land somewhere in the $285–$365 range for labor. Trim, tall or vaulted walls, heavy patching, and dark-over-light color changes all push it up. The honest answer is that nobody can price your room accurately from a website — that is what the free estimate is for. See how I put a number together.

Do you really have me buy the paint?

Yes, and it saves you money. Painting companies typically mark paint up 30–50%. When you buy it directly you pay store price, you pick the exact brand and sheen you want, and you keep the leftovers for touch-ups later.

You will not be guessing. After the walkthrough I give you an exact list — brand, product line, sheen, color, and how many gallons — so you buy the right thing once. If you would rather not deal with the store, tell me and we will work something out.

How long does painting a room take?

A single bedroom, walls only, is usually one day. Add trim and it is typically two. A whole main floor is normally three to five days depending on how much patching and how many colors are involved. I will give you a realistic day count in your estimate, and if something changes I will tell you before it becomes a surprise.

Do I need to move my furniture out?

No. Move small, fragile, and valuable things yourself — electronics, art, anything you would hate to lose. I will move the rest to the middle of the room, cover it, and cover the floors. Everything goes back where it was before I leave.

You are 19. Why should I trust you with my house?

Fair question, and I would rather answer it than dodge it. I have been painting since I was young — on job sites, on crews, doing the prep and the cut-in and the cleanup that nobody sees. What you are hiring is the hands that do the work, not a salesperson who hands your house to whoever is available that week.

Here is how I would suggest you protect yourself with any painter: ask for a written estimate, do not pay in full up front, and walk the finished job with them before the final payment. I do all three by default. More about who I am and how I work.

What areas do you serve?

I am based in Plymouth and work across the west metro — Maple Grove, Minnetonka, Wayzata, Golden Valley, Medina, New Hope, Crystal, Osseo, Orono, St. Louis Park, and the surrounding suburbs. See the full service area. If you are just outside it, ask anyway.

Everything else

The rest of it

How do I get an estimate?

Call or text (952) 486-0205, or send the form. I will get back to you, usually the same day, to set a time to come look at the space. The visit takes about 20 minutes. You get a written number, and there is no charge and no obligation.

Do you give estimates over the phone?

I can give you a rough range over the phone or from photos, and I am happy to. But I will not pretend it is a real quote. Wall condition, patching, ceiling height, and color changes move the number a lot, and I would rather see it than guess and be wrong in either direction.

How much prep work do you actually do?

Prep is most of the job and it is where cheap paint jobs fall apart. On a normal interior repaint that means: furniture moved and covered, floors covered, outlet and switch plates off, nail holes and dings filled and sanded smooth, glossy or stained spots spot-primed, and every edge cut in by hand. Caulk lines get touched up where trim meets wall. Then the paint goes on.

One coat or two?

Two coats, unless we agree otherwise for a specific reason. One coat over an existing similar color can look fine on the day and then show flashing and roller lap marks in a year. If your estimate says two coats, that is what you get.

What if I do not like the color once it is on the wall?

Color always reads differently on a wall than on a chip — that is normal, not a mistake. Buy a sample pot first and paint a two-foot square on the actual wall, then look at it in morning light and at night. I will tell you honestly if I think a color is going to fight the room. If you change your mind after a coat is on, repainting is additional work and I will quote it before doing it.

Do you paint ceilings?

Not right now. I keep my focus on interior walls and trim, which means every job gets full attention instead of being spread across five different specialties. If you need ceilings done too, ask me when we talk and I can point you toward someone who does them well, or we can time it so both happen back to back.

Can you paint kitchen cabinets?

Not right now. Cabinet refinishing done properly needs a spray setup and a controlled space, and I would rather tell you no than hand you a brushed cabinet job that chips in a year. Interior walls and trim are what I do.

Do you do exterior painting?

No — interior only. Keeping the focus narrow is deliberate. It means the work I do take on gets done properly instead of squeezed between three other kinds of jobs.

What about drywall damage?

Nail holes, dings, small dents, popped screws, and minor patches are part of a normal repaint and I handle them. Large holes, water damage, ceiling repairs, or anything structural is a different trade and I will tell you that up front rather than paint over it. More on what I patch.

How do you handle payment?

No deposit on small jobs. On larger projects I may ask for a deposit to reserve the dates, with the balance due when the job is finished and you have walked it with me and you are happy. Cash, check, Venmo, and Zelle all work. You get it in writing before anything starts.

Do you work weekends?

Saturdays, yes. Sundays I do not work — that day is for church and family. I would rather be upfront about that than be vague and cancel on you.

What happens if something gets damaged?

Tell me right away and I will make it right. That is exactly why I cover floors and furniture before anything opens, and why we walk the finished job together before you pay the balance.

Will there be a strong paint smell?

Much less than it used to be. Modern interior latex paints are low-VOC and the smell is usually gone within a day with normal ventilation. If anyone in the house is sensitive, or there is a baby or someone with asthma, tell me and I will recommend a zero-VOC product for your list.

How soon can you start?

It depends on the season. Spring and fall book up fastest in the Twin Cities; January through March is usually the easiest time to get on the calendar and the quietest time to have someone in your house. Ask and I will tell you honestly what is open.

Are you insured?

I want to give you a straight answer instead of a marketing one: True Painting MN is a new business and I am in the process of getting general liability coverage in place. Until I can tell you it is done, I am not going to claim it. If insurance is a requirement for your job — and for some homeowners and most HOAs it is — ask me directly and I will tell you exactly where I stand at that moment.

Do you paint homes built before 1978?

Ask me before you book. Homes built before 1978 can have lead-based paint underneath, and federal law puts specific requirements on contractors who disturb it. I will not cut corners on that or pretend the rule does not apply. Tell me the age of your home when you call and I will tell you straight whether I can take the job.
