What Plymouth houses are usually like

Plymouth grew in waves, and you can more or less date a neighborhood by driving through it. Each era comes with its own painting quirks.

The 1970s and 80s stock

Split-levels, split-entries and early two-stories, often on wooded lots south of County Road 6 and around the older lakes. These tend to have stained wood trim, sometimes wood paneling, and the original walls have usually been repainted a couple of times already. The most common request I get from these houses is taking the trim from dark stained wood to white — and it genuinely transforms them. More on how that's done properly.

The 1990s and 2000s stock

The big wave — two-story colonials and transitionals through the middle and north of the city, out toward Vicksburg and beyond. Nine-foot main floor ceilings became standard, entryways got tall, and honey oak trim was in nearly everything. These houses are now at the age where the original builder paint is tired, and the beige-on-beige palette they came with is the thing owners most want gone.

The newer developments

Northwest Plymouth kept building through the 2010s. These homes are still in decent shape paint-wise, but builder-grade flat white is thin stuff, marks easily, and can't be scrubbed. Repainting hallways, stairwells and kids' rooms in a washable eggshell or satin is one of the most practical upgrades you can make.

Buying or selling a house in Plymouth?

The two cheapest times to paint a house are the week before you list it and the week after you close and before the furniture arrives. An empty house has nothing to move and nothing to protect, so the same work costs less. If you're closing in a few weeks and the walls are dated, that gap is the best window you will ever get — call me while it's still open.

Winter is a genuinely good time to paint in Minnesota

Interior painting isn't weather-dependent, but everyone books it in spring and fall anyway. That means January through March is quiet, easier to get on the calendar, and a much less disruptive time to have someone working in your house — nobody's using the deck, nobody's got the windows open, and the kids are at school. Furnace-dry winter air also means paint cures faster. If your schedule is flexible, that's the time to ask.
