Stained oak trim to white: the biggest change you can make

If your house was built in the west metro between roughly 1985 and 2005, there's a good chance it came with honey oak trim throughout. It was everywhere. Painting it white is the single most dramatic thing you can do to a house of that era — rooms read brighter, taller and about fifteen years newer.

It is also real work, and I want to be honest with you about that before you get a number and flinch.

Why it costs more than repainting already-white trim

Stained trim is sealed with polyurethane or varnish. Paint will not bond to that surface on its own — it will look perfect for six months and then peel off in sheets the first time someone drags a vacuum along the baseboard. Doing it properly means:

  • Clean everything. Years of hand oils, cooking film and furniture polish have to come off or nothing sticks.
  • Scuff-sand every surface. Every piece of casing, every baseboard, by hand, to give the primer something to grab.
  • Bonding primer. A proper adhesion primer made for slick and glossy surfaces — not a general-purpose wall primer.
  • Fill the grain, if you want it smooth. Oak has deep open grain. Paint alone leaves that texture visible. If you want a modern flat look, that grain has to be filled and sanded first. Some people like the grain showing through; some hate it. Tell me which you are before I quote it.
  • Two finish coats, brushed carefully. Trim is at eye level and gets looked at closely.

That's real time on a normal main floor. Anyone quoting you a stained-trim-to-white job as a quick add-on is planning to skip one of those steps, and you'll find out which one in about a year.

What sheen to use

Semi-gloss is the traditional choice and still the most practical. It wipes clean, resists scuffs from shoes and vacuums, and gives trim a crisp definition against the wall.

Satin is softer and more current. It hides imperfections in older trim better and reads less shiny, which some people much prefer. On trim that has been painted many times over decades, satin is often the kinder choice.

Either is fine. I'll show you both on your actual trim before you commit.
