Doing several rooms at once is meaningfully cheaper

A large chunk of any paint job is setup and breakdown — covering, taping, moving furniture, hauling gear in and out. Do three rooms in one visit instead of three separate visits and you pay for that setup once. If you're thinking about the hallway "eventually," it's almost always cheaper to do it now.

What actually moves the price

Two rooms that look identical to you can be very different jobs. These are the things I'm looking at during the estimate, and the reason I'd rather see the space than guess over the phone.

Things that push the price up

  • Tall or vaulted ceilings. Ladder work and staging is slower and less safe than standing on the floor, and there's simply more wall.
  • Dark to light. Covering a deep red, navy or charcoal usually means a primer coat before the two finish coats.
  • Stained wood trim going white. Cleaning, scuff-sanding and bonding primer on every piece. It's a real transformation and it's real work.
  • Heavy patching. A wall full of anchor holes, dents, or old wallpaper glue takes hours before any paint goes on.
  • Lots of cut-in. Accent walls, built-ins, wainscoting, many windows — anywhere a brush has to go slowly.
  • Rooms that are hard to set up in. Wall-to-wall furniture, a full closet system, or a room you need to keep using.

Things that bring it down

  • Doing several rooms in one visit. Setup gets paid for once instead of three times.
  • Walls only. Skipping trim is the single biggest lever.
  • Staying in the same color family. Less coverage work.
  • An empty or nearly empty room. A house you haven't moved into yet is the cheapest house to paint.
  • Booking in the slow season. January through March is quiet in the Twin Cities.

Why my price is lower than a big company's

It isn't because I skip coats. It's overhead. There's no office, no commissioned salesperson, no branded truck fleet, and no project manager taking a cut between you and the person holding the brush. On top of that, you're buying the paint yourself at store price instead of paying a 30–50% markup on it inside a quote.

What doesn't change: the prep, the patching, the primer where it's needed, and two full coats.

How I charge

By the job, not by the hour. You get one written number before anything starts. If we find something behind the furniture that neither of us knew about, I stop and talk to you about it before doing extra work — you'll never get a bill with a surprise line on it.
