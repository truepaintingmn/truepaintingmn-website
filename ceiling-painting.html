Why fresh walls make your ceiling look worse

This catches almost everyone. Your ceiling has been slowly going warm and yellow for fifteen years and you never noticed, because it aged at the same rate as the walls next to it. Put crisp new paint on those walls and suddenly the ceiling looks grey, dingy and slightly beige by comparison.

That's why I always ask at the estimate. Doing the ceiling while the room is already covered and taped costs a fraction of what it costs to come back and do it as its own job later.

Water stains

An ice dam, a bathroom overflow upstairs, an old roof leak — the Twin Cities produces a lot of ceiling stains. Two rules:

  • The leak has to be fixed and the area has to be dry first. I'm not going to paint over an active problem and let you think it's handled.
  • The stain needs a stain-blocking primer, not more ceiling paint. Regular latex over a water stain will let the tannins bleed straight back through, usually within a few weeks. Then you're paying twice.

I roll ceilings, I don't spray them

Spraying is faster, and in an empty new-construction house it's the right call. In a home you live in, it means masking off every wall, window and floor in the room, and any gap in that masking becomes overspray on something you care about. Rolling is slower and gives a more controlled result with far less risk to your house.

Textured and popcorn ceilings

I don't scrape or remove popcorn texture. Textured ceilings installed before the mid-1980s can contain asbestos, and disturbing them is work for a licensed abatement contractor, not a painter. Anyone who offers to scrape yours as a casual add-on is someone to be careful with.

If your textured ceiling is sound and just needs paint, tell me the age of the house and we'll talk about whether it's a good candidate.

Flat, and only flat

Ceiling paint should be dead flat. Any sheen at all catches light across a wide flat plane and shows every roller mark, every seam and every tiny imperfection in the drywall above your head. Flat scatters that light and hides it. This is one of the few places in a house where there's really only one right answer.

Do you paint over water stains?

Only after the leak is fixed and the area is dry, and only with a stain-blocking primer first. Regular ceiling paint over a water stain will let the stain bleed right back through within weeks.

Do you spray or roll ceilings?

I roll. Spraying is faster but requires masking off the entire room, and in an occupied home rolling gives a cleaner, more controlled result with far less overspray risk to your floors and furniture.

Can you paint a popcorn or textured ceiling?

I do not scrape or remove popcorn texture. Textured ceilings installed before the mid-1980s can contain asbestos and that is work for a licensed abatement contractor, not a painter. If your textured ceiling is sound and just needs paint, ask me and we will talk about whether it is a good candidate.
